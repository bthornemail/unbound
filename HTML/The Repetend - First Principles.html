The Repetend

A fraction's decimal repeats; a square root's continued fraction repeats. Both are the orbit of a whole-number step on a finite set of states. This page sets out, from the first design decision up to Euclid I.3, how the record arrived at that, what each step rests on, and what is checked.

Each principle ends with three lines. Classical: what mathematics has known, and since when. Checked: what the record computes and where. Reading: what is the author's, held open. The arithmetic is old; the synthesis, binding it to the delta law, the breadboard, the scripts and the directorate, is the author's.

One design decision

The delta law, rotl(x,1) ⊕ rotl(x,3) ⊕ rotr(x,2) ⊕ C, masked to its width. Four choices: rotations (no bit is lost), XOR (reversible), a constant (no fixed point at zero), a mask (the state stays bounded). Everything else is computed: on 16 bits the law has period 8; the smallest prime whose decimal repeats with period 8 is 73; 1/73 repeats 01369863, the block B, summing to W = 36; a position's orbit and offset are divmod(position, 36).

A count is exact; a measure is not

The first harmonic signature took the hypotenuse of a text's bytes, h = √(Σb²). For GOD IS WORD, h = 235.2233…, never whole; h² = 55,330 always is. The first slide rule read its slot through the needle's angle and lost one slot every cycle (184.5° gave 122.99999999999999 where the count is 123): 5,040 ticks gave 5,033 slots. Through the count, all 5,040 come. So the record measures a value once, at the incidence, and counts everywhere else, as the π proof does: its terms are whole (n mod 7, n mod 240, 2n + 1) and only the limit is real.

Every fraction repeats: the repetend

Long division of 1 by q is the step r → 10r mod q on the q possible remainders; the digits repeat when a remainder does. The repeating block is the repetend; for q with no factor 2 or 5, its length is the order of 10 modulo q, the least k with q dividing 10k − 1 (factors of 2 and 5 only delay the start of the repetition).

The lengths are the circuits and the scripts

The breadboard's four XOR circuits have 5, 6, 8 and 10 transistors; the repetends of 1/41, 1/7, 1/73 and 1/9091 have lengths 5, 6, 8 and 10. Braille's 6-dot cell has six dots and 1/7 six digits; its 8-dot cell eight, and 1/73 eight. The Aegean numerals write base ten: the clean cycle repeats ten digits with zero, and with nine nines nine without, as an Aegean place has nine signs and no zero, in five places, as 1/41 repeats in five.

Collation is precedence

Every square root repeats too: the natural repetend

Euclid I.3 cuts off from the greater line a line equal to the less. On the triangular grid the cut lies at the fraction √(N(CD)/N(AB)) of AB: a grid point only when that is rational. A root has no repeating decimal. But its continued fraction repeats: the step

m′ = d·a − m,  d′ = (n − m′²)/d,  a′ = ⌊(a₀ + m′)/d′⌋

from (0, 1, a₀) runs on fewer than 2n states and closes its period on a = 2a₀. The convergent just before the close satisfies Pell's equation p² − n q² = (−1)L: a whole-number certificate that the period is right. √13 repeats with period 5, √19 with 6, √31 with 8, √43 with 10: the same lengths again.

The self-dual slide rule

Put the two recurrences side by side. Long division takes a fraction to its digits and repeats; the continued-fraction step takes a root to its terms and repeats. Each is a whole-number step on a finite state; each turns what cannot be written exactly in a few symbols (a ratio's decimal, a root's value) into a finite repeating block that can. One works on the rationals, the other on the square roots that the rationals cannot reach; between them they cover the lengths of Euclid I.1 to I.3. They do not cover everything: later constructions take roots of roots (the regular pentagon of Book IV, much of Book X), and those continued fractions do not repeat. That is the open edge, stated so it can be tested. That is the author's slide rule: meta-circular, because the rule is read by the same kind of step it measures, and self-dual, because each side does for its numbers what the other does for its own, as the transliteration does for a code point what the repetend does for a fraction.
